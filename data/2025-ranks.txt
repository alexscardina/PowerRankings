Preface: You know the drill by now. None of this actually matters, it’s all just dumb opinions and trash talk, some will age well and some won’t, and nothing that I say can be traced back to me in any meaningful way unless it’s right.

Season Retrospective
Well gentlemen, we’ve finally made it. The new year is right around the corner, and our 17-week descent into madness is complete. Both congratulations and commiserations are due to the Smith brothers, who fulfilled their destinies as the regular season winner and loser to then become our postseason winner and loser as well. 

Now, with the power of hindsight, it’s only fair we look back at what I previously wrote about all of these teams throughout the year, and give you all the opportunity to dunk on me and let me know what I did and did not get right about you or your team. Join me in celebrating, commiserating, and humiliating the decisions that led us to where the 2025 fantasy season ended.

12. Cyborg Squad (Austin)
Record: 9-19
Median Wins: 4
Playoff Performance: 6th in Loser’s Bracket 💩
Draft Recap Ranking: 12th 
Mid-Season Ranking: 12th
Playoff Pred. Ranking: 12th
Best Player: Zay Flowers - 5th Round Pick (WR12 overall, WR19 in PPG) 
Worst Player: James Conner - 3rd Round Pick (RB75 overall, season-ending ankle injury)

What I wrote in September, about the Machine breaking halfway through the draft: “Maybe you should just take this as an omen, Aug.” Boy, could that not have been more correct.

Doing these rankings can be a pain in the ass sometimes, since there are always multiple teams you could justify putting in every single spot. Then, every so often, a gem like Austin’s team comes around, and it makes my job just that little bit easier knowing I don’t have to work hard for at least this one. This one is nice. It is ultimately fitting and just that the team who was 12th in the beginning, midpoint, and end rankings, and also the regular season standings, ends up as our playoff loser. It wouldn’t have felt right any other way. The team’s best player was a WR who scored 3 total touchdowns on the season.

This season will not be remembered fondly, and the most impactful moves made were the ones made not by a machine, but by the man himself. Trading away Derrick Henry for three starters seemed okay at the time, but it didn’t stop Austin from getting last place in the regular season, and Henry’s playoff performances likely would have seen Austin escape the doubling of his last-place punishment. On top of that, Austin spent almost 100% of his FAAB on Tre Tucker, and ended up starting him for the majority of the season. Since Tucker’s 36.9-point performance in week 3, he scored double-digit points one time, and was the WR67 (👀) in points per game. The moves were bad, the team was consistently at the bottom of the league, and Austin got what he deserved. At least the season wasn’t the WORST it could have been, since he didn’t have to give his cold hard cash to his bitter rival and brother… right?

What I wrote in September: ““Oooh look at me, I’m Connor, I just had an amazing wedding with all of my friends and family and now I’m travelling the world with my wife who loves me and I just won fantasy last year and I had the best draft this year despite the fact that I was drafting from a hotel in southwestern Africa” man fuck you.” I stand by that.

Connor’s team turned from the best in the league, to one of the worst in the league, to the most fraudulent in the league, to the championship favorites, to the eventual runners up, just single-digit points away from winning it all. I mean I don’t even think he could have predicted how close he ended up getting, and also how much he fell off from week 15 to the finale. A bizarre ride the entire way that truly encapsulates fantasy football at its best. 

Up until the playoffs, Connor’s team was 8th in total points and had more median losses than wins in the previous 10 weeks. And, taking away week 15, the trend of underperforming relative to expectations continued for this team. It’s just that an absolutely nuclear, incredibly special, borderline unprecedented playoff round 1 performance catapulted this team from playoff frauds to championship hopefuls in an instant. I don’t think the players even knew what to do when they got to that point, which could explain the eventual collapse.

In trying to find this team’s worst or most disappointing player, we could talk about Justin Jefferson and how he proved he is not in fact QB-proof and from week 10 onward he’s had one game where he surpassed double digit fantasy points. Or we could talk about 4th-round pick Chuba Hubbard getting hurt and then immediately losing his starting job to a guy who had 200+ scrimmage yards in back-to-back games. But, when discussing this season, there’s really one failure that stands above them all, and that’s the Chris Olave for Drake London trade that ultimately ruined Connor’s championship hopes. I respect what Connor did here, attempting to move a good WR and some depth for a top-5 option to strengthen the roster for the playoffs. But boy did it not work. Olave was the WR2 from week 13 to week 17, only behind Puka Nacua, and London only played 2 games in that span and amassed a total of 5.1 points, including the final Monday Night Football game of the season where Connor needed 12 points and London couldn’t even muster 1. Just brutal.

I hope this is a season that Connor will remember fondly; a great start with a great draft followed by an improbable playoff run and eventual podium placing is still nothing to sneeze at. We’ll always have those Trevor Lawrence goal line rushing TDs at Cameron’s place, they can never take that away from us.

This season for Cam was almost exactly what he expected, but to the nth degree; the first three picks of Cam’s draft established a very solid floor, and allowed the rest of the players room for error in case there were weeks they didn’t perform. Now, I certainly didn’t expect CMC to be this consistent (and this healthy) all year, and I wasn’t expecting this many great games from Jacobs either, nor was I expecting Egbuka to be one of the best draft picks in fantasy for the first half of the season. However, things kind of fell apart as the season progressed. I kept mentioning how Cam’s team lacked depth, and the back-end starters inspired little confidence. Rhamondre Stevenson, Marvin Harrison, Zach Charbonnet, Devonta Smith; all guys that really couldn’t put it together for more than a week or two at a time, and you never knew what you were getting out of them. 

The other thing that truly defined Cam’s season was in-season management; it seemed that at literally every opportunity, Cameron started the wrong player, and it cost him points/wins in the process. I don’t know if we’ve ever seen a worse manager performance regarding start/sit decisions in the league’s history. FantasyPros gives Cam a 75% start/sit accuracy for the season, which is the league’s lowest, and I think that’s what will truly eat away at Cam when he remembers this year. 

And unfortunately, the season ended with a whimper. It helps to contextualize Cameron’s playoff performance by emphasizing that his worst and least-reliable scorer, Kyle Pitts, had a 40-point performance in the first week of the playoffs, and Christian McCaffrey was the top-scoring RB in weeks 15-17, and even with those scores, Cam still couldn’t finish inside this season’s top 3 playoff performances. Maybe Cam will sit awake at night wondering what could have been, had just a few more things gone right.
